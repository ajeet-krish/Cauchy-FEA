L-Bracket Stress Concentration

An L-shaped bracket with a re-entrant corner that produces a stress concentration. This case demonstrates the need for mesh refinement toward high-stress regions and the behavior of singular stress fields.

Problem Setup

ParameterValue
Domain[0, 2] x [0, 2]
Cutout[1, 2] x [0, 1] (bottom-right removed)
MaterialSteel: E=200 GPa, nu=0.3, t=0.01m
Top edge (y=2)uy=0 (fixed)
Left edge (x=0)ux=0 (fixed)
Left-bottom load-1000N distributed on left edge below corner
Mesh32x32 structured with L-bracket cutout

Discussion

The re-entrant corner at (1, 1) produces a theoretical stress singularity: the stress approaches infinity as the mesh is refined. This means the maximum stress does NOT converge with h-refinement, which is expected behavior for singular geometries. The L-bracket case demonstrates why mesh convergence studies must be interpreted carefully -- a diverging stress does not indicate a bug, but rather a fundamental limitation of linear elasticity at sharp corners.

In practice, real brackets have fillets at re-entrant corners. The stress concentration factor depends on the fillet radius. This case uses a sharp corner to exercise the solver's behavior near singularities.
